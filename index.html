The argument in one line

AI is not a stranger,
and it isn’t a god.

It’s a mirror. And the work was never about the machine.

Every business leader is expected to have a point of view on artificial intelligence, and most are working from a mix of hype, fear, and half-understood headlines. Born From Us cuts through both the utopian and doomsday narratives to explain what AI actually is, where it genuinely changes the game, and where it doesn’t.

Drawing on real examples, including from life sciences and healthcare, Ritesh Dogra and Priya Setty debunk the most common myths leaders carry into boardrooms, and offer a way to ask sharper questions instead of chasing simple answers.

The through-line: the three questions that survive every room

  • 01What is it doing?
  • 02What is it reflecting?
  • 03Who’s responsible when it’s wrong?

Inside the book

Twenty-five short, story-driven chapters.
Five parts. No jargon required.

It draws on real, specific stories from healthcare, regulatory work, parenting, aviation, and ordinary life, not because the lessons are industry-specific, but because they aren’t. If you run a business, you’ll recognize every one of them.

The authors

Two people, the same three questions.

Ritesh Dogra

PGDM, Indian School of Business · Executive education, IIM Ahmedabad and Harvard Business School

Ritesh Dogra has spent nearly two decades doing the same essential work in different settings: finding what actually works when domain expertise meets an emerging technology, and building the thing that proves it. He founded TalkDoc.AI in 2020, one of the first Made-in-India NLP platforms built for healthcare, built Indegene’s GenAI practice from the ground up into a capability serving a dozen-plus global life sciences clients, and in late 2025 co-founded Alethic Insights, where he builds AI-native products for enterprise clients today.

Priya Setty

MBA · PMP · RAC-Devices

Geethapriya “Priya” Setty started as a pediatric occupational therapist, watching how children build judgment slowly, through hundreds of small situations, rather than by rule. That lens followed her into medical-device regulatory affairs, where she has spent years on global submissions, quality systems, and, for the last several years, AI governance and validation in regulated healthcare. She’s a Global Regulatory Affairs Manager, holds an MBA, a PMP, and RAC-Devices credentials, and lives in San Diego with her husband and two kids, who can deflate any sufficiently abstract sentence about AI within about four seconds, usually by asking if dinner’s ready.

Edited by

Astha Sharma

Astha Sharma is a case author and researcher at the Indian School of Business (ISB), where she has co-authored multiple business and management case studies distributed through Harvard Business Publishing. She works closely with ISB’s research centers and faculty, often collaborating with management experts including Professor D.V.R. Seshadri, and her writing centers on real-world business challenges in emerging markets: digital transformation, healthcare innovation, and strategic disruption. Her cases include Scaling Digital Transformation: Growing LVPEI’s eyeSmart EMR, on how the L.V. Prasad Eye Institute scaled its electronic medical record system, and Reliance Jio: Ultimate Datagiri, on Jio’s entry into Indian telecom. She brought that same eye, for where the real story is and where it’s easy to miss, to editing this manuscript.
